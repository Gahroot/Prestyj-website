Use AI to assemble a dated funding recommendation from property cash forecasts, committed capex, debt events and verified borrowing availability. Require a reproducible cash bridge, source attached, and a review gate before issuing any capital-call notice.

## What should the capital-call recommendation contain?

Define the finished work product before choosing how to prepare it. Ask for a decision package, not just a forecast chart.

Require the recommendation to state:

- **Amount:** proposed capital to call, separated by purpose.
- **Timing:** notice date, contribution due date and the first cash obligation requiring funding.
- **Cash protection:** lowest projected unrestricted cash balance against an approved reserve.
- **Debt treatment:** property-debt repayments, refinancing proceeds, subscription-line draws and repayments.
- **Alternatives:** an earlier call, a permitted bridge or an approved spending deferral.
- **Conditions:** unresolved items that could change the recommendation.
- **Authority:** named reviewers and the person authorized to release notices.

ILPA’s Capital Call & Distribution Template is intended to make the accounting detail and transaction components of calls and distributions visible in a standardized format. Use that principle when designing the handoff from the funding recommendation to the notice package.

Keep the recommendation separate from authorization. Ask the CFO to approve funding strategy and the administrator to validate investor allocations before notice release.

## How should property cash needs become fund-level funding requirements?

Start with a dated event register. Require each entry to identify the paying entity, receiving entity, amount, cash date, evidence, reviewer and inclusion status.

Use these checks before rolling anything up:

| Input | Recommended review rule |
|---|---|
| Property operating forecast | Separate cash retained at the property from cash approved for upstream transfer. |
| Committed capex | Tie remaining payments to approved commitments and payment dates; reconcile them to forecast capex already included. |
| Property debt | Record maturity payments, amortization, refinancing proceeds and closing costs separately. |
| Fund expenses | Identify the paying entity and payment date; reconcile against expenses already included elsewhere. |
| Subscription facility | Separate outstanding borrowings, verified additional availability, costs and repayment obligations. |
| Investor commitments | Ask the administrator and counsel to validate callable amounts and allocation restrictions. |

Do not simply add every property’s forecast deficit to the fund model. First ask whether the fund must supply that cash, whether another entity bears a share, and when the transfer must occur.

Likewise, do not count property cash as available fund liquidity until the reviewer has approved the transfer amount and timing. Keep restricted cash outside the available-cash calculation unless the responsible reviewer confirms its permitted use.

For capex, classify each remaining payment as committed, approved but uncommitted, or discretionary. Make any proposed deferral explicit; do not let a scenario silently defer a contractual payment.

For debt, require the gross repayment and proposed refinancing receipt to remain visible. Do not replace them with a net funding amount until treasury has reviewed both dates and the conditions supporting the receipt.

Where records conflict, resolve the input before approving the call. Use the review discipline described in [When the model and property system disagree](/blog/reconciling-property-data-conflicts).

## How should the workflow calculate the amount and notice date?

Use AI-assisted work preparation for evidence extraction, event classification and drafting. Require arithmetic to run through an approved, reproducible calculation rather than accepting a narrative answer.

For each cash date, calculate:

**Projected closing cash = opening unrestricted cash + approved receipts + capital contributions + permitted borrowing − payments − debt repayments.**

Set an approved minimum cash reserve. Then size the contribution to keep projected cash above that reserve throughout the selected horizon—not merely at the horizon’s end.

Require the calculation to:

- Reconcile opening cash to the approved treasury position.
- Preserve gross debt flows before calculating net equity needs.
- Distinguish expected cash from cash approved for inclusion.
- Eliminate internal transfers in consolidated totals while preserving entity-level funding requirements.
- Show the lowest cash balance and its date.
- Recalculate under approved downside assumptions.

Work backward from the first funding deadline. Ask counsel and the administrator to confirm the applicable notice provisions, delivery requirements, calendar convention and investor-specific terms. Then add the treasury-approved collection and settlement allowance.

NIST’s AI Risk Management Framework calls for policies that distinguish human responsibilities within human–AI arrangements and AI oversight. Apply that distinction here: identify who prepares, who checks the calculation and who authorizes the call.

If the available notice window is insufficient, route the issue for an approved funding decision. Do not allow the workflow to assume a shorter notice period.

## How should subscription-line availability affect the recommendation?

Treat the line as a separately reviewed funding alternative, not an automatic reduction in the capital call.

ILPA’s subscription-line guidance recommends clearer partnership-agreement parameters governing facility use. Ask counsel to confirm the applicable permissions and limits before including a proposed draw.

Require treasury to validate:

- Additional availability as of the proposed draw date.
- The relevant borrowing-base calculation and draw conditions.
- Facility maturity and the proposed repayment date.
- Permitted uses, costs and any required approvals.
- The capital contribution or other approved receipt supporting repayment.

Show both the draw and its eventual repayment. Reject any comparison that lowers the near-term call by adding borrowing but omits the resulting repayment obligation.

ILPA’s follow-on guidance recommends quarterly and annual disclosures covering subscription-line exposure, performance effects, key terms and costs. Preserve those categories in the supporting funding schedule.

Ask the reviewer to compare a direct capital call with a permitted bridge through the same horizon, including repayment. Keep any decision to preserve borrowing capacity explicit.

### Synthetic recommendation and timing

The synthetic unfinanced low point is **negative $3.0 million on February 12, 2027**. Adding the synthetic **$2.0 million** reserve produces a proposed synthetic call of **$5.0 million**.

Recommend receiving that synthetic contribution by **January 14, 2027**, before the synthetic capex payment breaches the reserve.

Assume the synthetic governing documents permit a **10-calendar-day** notice period. Subject to completed reviews, recommend issuing the synthetic notice on **January 4, 2027**, with contributions due **January 14, 2027**. That leaves the synthetic settlement allowance of **one calendar day** before the capex payment.

### Synthetic borrowing alternative and downside

Assume a synthetic facility commitment of **$6.0 million**, existing borrowings of **$2.5 million**, and treasury-verified additional availability of **$1.5 million**. Do not substitute the synthetic unused commitment of **$3.5 million** for verified availability.

Assume any synthetic incremental draw must also be repaid by **February 12, 2027**. Present it as a timing alternative, not a reduction in the synthetic **$5.0 million** contribution requirement through that repayment date.

For a synthetic downside, reduce refinancing proceeds from **$6.0 million to $4.0 million**. The additional synthetic equity requirement is **$2.0 million**, increasing the proposed synthetic call to **$7.0 million**.

The synthetic approval question should therefore be specific: approve the base call only after validating refinancing funding, or approve the larger downside call and document the reserve decision.

## What should stop the recommendation at the review gate?

Define blocking conditions before preparing the package. Send unresolved items to an exception queue with an owner, resolution deadline and affected funding decision.

Block release when:

- Opening cash does not reconcile to the approved treasury position.
- A capex commitment appears in both a property forecast and an incremental funding request.
- A proposed upstream transfer lacks entity-level approval.
- Refinancing proceeds lack sufficient support for the funding assumption.
- Borrowing availability is stale or draw conditions remain unresolved.
- The contribution due date fails the reviewed notice requirements.
- Allocations do not reconcile to the approved call amount.
- A scenario changes the call without identifying the changed assumption.

Require changes after approval to show the previous recommendation, revised amount or date, supporting evidence and renewed approval requirement.

For ownership and escalation design, see [Start with exception queues, not a chatbot](/blog/ai-for-real-estate-investment-funds-exception-queues). Keep this queue focused on decisions that affect funding amount, timing or authority.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should we call capital for all budgeted capex?

Do not use the entire budget as the automatic call amount. Ask reviewers to separate dated commitments, approved uncommitted work and discretionary spending. Include the approved funding requirement within the selected horizon, with any contingency separately authorized.

### Can expected refinancing proceeds reduce the call?

Permit that treatment only after treasury approves the supporting evidence, conditions and cash timing. Require a downside without the disputed proceeds, and show the resulting contribution requirement and notice deadline.

### Should available subscription borrowing reduce the capital call?

Compare the complete draw-and-repayment path with a direct call. Require the recommendation to explain whether borrowing changes contribution timing, contribution amount within the horizon, or neither.

### What should the fund administrator receive?

Send the approved amount, purposes, notice date, due date, allocation instructions, supporting cash bridge and approval record. Ask the administrator to validate investor-level calculations and return exceptions before notices are released.

## Review and limitations

Assign property finance to review operating cash and capex; treasury to review balances, debt events and borrowing availability; and fund finance to reconcile the consolidated bridge. Ask counsel to review contractual interpretations and the administrator to validate allocations and notice preparation. Reserve final authorization for the designated fund approver.

NIST’s framework also calls for human-oversight processes to be defined, evaluated and documented. Record the review scope and decision, rather than treating an approval checkbox as sufficient evidence.
