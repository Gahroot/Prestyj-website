Answering the phone is not the outcome. The outcome is a broker receiving the right inquiry, with enough context to act, while the prospect is still moving.

## Define the qualification record

A commercial inquiry may need property, market, use, size, timing, budget, representation status, financing, and next-step availability. The exact fields depend on the assignment.

A voice agent should qualify against those approved criteria, not improvise a generic sales script.

## Preserve consent and disclosure

The caller should know they are interacting with an automated agent. Recording and follow-up must follow the consent and communications rules that apply to the call. The system should record the consent state with the interaction.

## Route on business rules

Routing can depend on property, geography, assignment team, language, urgency, or transaction type. If a rule is ambiguous or the caller requests a person, the agent should escalate instead of forcing completion.

## Hand back the complete record

The broker should receive:

- caller identity and contact details;
- the qualification fields collected;
- the full transcript or structured call notes;
- recording-consent status where applicable;
- classification and urgency;
- scheduled next step;
- unanswered questions;
- the reason for the routing decision.

A phone number without context only moves the coordination burden downstream.

## Test failure paths

Test poor audio, interruptions, missing consent, duplicate CRM records, unavailable calendars, unsupported questions, and requests for a human. The agent should fail closed and preserve the caller's progress.

## Measure the handoff

Useful measures include answered inquiries, completed qualification, correct routing, scheduled next steps, broker acceptance, and exceptions requiring review. A fast response is valuable only when the handoff remains accurate.
