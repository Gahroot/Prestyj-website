**TL;DR:** **Do not estimate missed-call losses from a universal industry percentage. Export one cohort of eligible inbound callers, split it by answered versus unanswered first touch, calculate collected gross profit per caller for each group, and subtract the fully loaded cost of any proposed fix. Treat the observed gap as an association until a controlled pilot confirms it.**

**Direct answer:** The cost of an unanswered call is not the job value. Measure the difference in collected gross profit per eligible caller between comparable answered-first-touch and unanswered-first-touch cohorts, then multiply that gap by the number of unanswered callers. Use the result as a diagnostic—not a guaranteed recovery forecast.

| Input | Definition | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Eligible inbound callers** | Unique, valid sales or service callers in the measurement window | Phone-system export |
| **2. Unanswered first touches** | Eligible callers not connected to a qualified person or approved agent on the first attempt | Call disposition log |
| **3. Answered-cohort gross profit** | Collected gross profit attributed to answered-first-touch callers | CRM plus accounting |
| **4. Unanswered-cohort gross profit** | Collected gross profit attributed to unanswered-first-touch callers | CRM plus accounting |
| **5. Cohort caller counts** | Unique callers in each group after spam, duplicates, and existing-customer service calls are handled consistently | Joined call and CRM export |
| **6. Fully loaded solution cost** | Subscription, usage, telephony, setup amortization, integration, monitoring, and human oversight | Vendor quote plus internal labor |
| **7. Recovery measured in a pilot** | Additional collected gross profit versus a comparable baseline or holdout | CRM, accounting, and experiment log |

## How Do You Define an Unanswered Call?

Write the definition before opening the data. Otherwise, the result will change whenever someone wants a larger or smaller number.

For this worksheet, an **unanswered first touch** is an eligible inbound caller who did not connect with a qualified person or an approved automated agent during the first call. Decide in advance how to handle:

- calls abandoned before a minimum ring duration;
- spam, robocalls, wrong numbers, and vendor calls;
- repeat calls from the same person;
- existing-customer support calls versus new opportunities;
- calls transferred but disconnected before a qualified conversation;
- voicemail answered later in the same measurement window; and
- after-hours calls when the business intentionally offers no coverage.

Keep the raw disposition and the normalized category. Do not relabel a voicemail as a completed conversation.

## What Formula Measures the Opportunity Cost?

Calculate collected gross profit per eligible caller for both cohorts:

```text
answered gross profit per caller
  = answered-cohort collected gross profit ÷ answered-cohort callers

unanswered gross profit per caller
  = unanswered-cohort collected gross profit ÷ unanswered-cohort callers

observed gross-profit gap per caller
  = answered gross profit per caller − unanswered gross profit per caller

monthly associated opportunity
  = unanswered-first-touch callers × observed gross-profit gap per caller
```

Use **collected gross profit**, not quoted revenue, booked revenue, or average ticket. Gross profit keeps fulfillment cost in the model; collected results exclude unsigned estimates and canceled jobs.

If the gap is negative, report it. Do not force the worksheet to justify a purchase.

### What Does an Illustrative Calculation Look Like?

Assume a company exports the following **fictional inputs solely to demonstrate the formula**:

- 800 eligible inbound callers;
- 600 answered on first touch and 200 unanswered;
- $54,000 collected gross profit from the answered cohort; and
- $7,000 collected gross profit from the unanswered cohort.

The answered cohort produced $90 in collected gross profit per caller. The unanswered cohort produced $35 per caller. The observed gap is $55; multiplied by 200 unanswered callers, that is an **associated** $11,000 monthly opportunity.

That calculation does **not** prove that answering every call would recover $11,000. Call time, source, intent, service area, staffing, and caller behavior can differ between cohorts.

## How Do You Avoid Overstating the Result?

Apply these controls before presenting the number:

1. **Match the windows.** Compare cohorts from the same dates and use enough lag for jobs to close and revenue to be collected.
2. **Segment the data.** Compare the same lead source, service line, geography, daypart, and new-versus-existing-customer status.
3. **Deduplicate callers.** Use one person or household as the unit, not raw call attempts.
4. **Separate association from causation.** Faster answering may correlate with better outcomes while source quality or staffing explains part of the gap.
5. **Keep cancellations and refunds.** Use realized gross profit after cancellations, clawbacks, and refunds.
6. **Document exclusions.** Retain the query, spreadsheet, and reason for every excluded call.

A vendor case study, generic missed-call rate, or platform-wide average cannot replace the company's own joined call, CRM, and accounting data.

## How Should You Compare a Receptionist, Answering Service, and AI Agent?

Collect the same cost and operating evidence for every option:

| Cost or control | What to verify |
| --- | --- |
| Subscription and minimums | Contracted monthly fee, included usage, overages, and annual commitment |
| Telephony | Carrier, transfer, recording, transcription, and number costs |
| Setup and integration | One-time implementation plus internal CRM/calendar work |
| Human oversight | QA, escalation, script maintenance, exception handling, and reporting time |
| Coverage | Exact channels, hours, overflow behavior, and outage fallback |
| Outcome definition | Qualified conversation, appointment, completed appointment, and collected gross profit |
| Compliance | Consent, recording notice, retention, suppression, and market-specific review |

Do not compare a salary with a software subscription headline. Compare fully loaded cost at the same coverage and scope.

## What Is the Break-Even Formula?

```text
monthly net benefit
  = pilot-measured recovered gross profit − fully loaded monthly solution cost

break-even recovered jobs
  = fully loaded monthly solution cost ÷ collected gross profit per recovered job
```

Use the company's actual collected gross profit per recovered job. Run downside cases for lower contact, booking, show, close, and collection rates. A result below zero is a valid reason not to expand the system.

## How Do You Run a 30-Day Pilot?

1. Freeze the unanswered-call definition and success metrics.
2. Export a baseline cohort with enough time for outcomes to mature.
3. Choose a narrow source, service line, or coverage window for the pilot.
4. Preserve a comparable baseline or holdout where operationally and ethically appropriate.
5. Track connection, qualification, booking, completed appointment, collected revenue, gross profit, opt-outs, complaints, and escalations.
6. Reconcile CRM outcomes with accounting before calculating return.
7. Expand only if the measured gross-profit recovery exceeds fully loaded cost without unacceptable customer or compliance failures.

Do not claim payback from appointments alone. An appointment that cancels, no-shows, or never produces collected gross profit does not fund the system.
