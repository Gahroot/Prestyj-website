Real estate teams do not need an AI agent that simply sends “Are you still interested?” They need a system that understands lead source, property or market context, timeline, representation, financing stage, routing rules, and when a licensed person must take over.

**Direct answer:** Teams under 50 monthly leads usually need instant appointment response tied to ads and a calendar. Teams processing 50–250 leads need website chat, SMS qualification, and database reactivation. Teams above 250 leads, with several markets or offices, need voice, receptionist, and more sophisticated routing. The agent should create qualified conversations for licensed agents—not provide legal, financial, or representation advice.

## The real estate workflows that matter

### Portal and paid-lead response

Pass source, campaign, property, location, and form details into the conversation. The agent should not force a buyer to explain which listing or offer triggered the inquiry.

### Buyer qualification

Use approved questions for area, timing, price range, financing stage, property type, and current representation. Keep steering, protected-class, and neighborhood questions out of automated recommendation logic.

### Seller qualification

Collect property address, timeline, reason for considering a move, occupancy, and preferred consultation time. Do not invent valuations or promise a sale price.

### Calendar and round-robin routing

Route by market, language, lead type, shift, agent availability, and existing relationships. Preserve the original source and conversation summary in the CRM.

### Long-term nurture

A lead who says “six months” should not receive daily appointment requests. Update timeline and use an appropriate, consent-aware follow-up sequence.

### Database reactivation

Real estate CRMs contain old portal leads, open-house contacts, past clients, and incomplete inquiries. Segment before outreach. A past client campaign should not sound like a cold buyer campaign.

Use AI to create and preserve opportunities. Use people to advise, negotiate, show property, price, and close.

## Compliance and fair-housing guardrails

Real estate workflows need explicit constraints. Obtain qualified legal and brokerage guidance for your markets.

- Do not steer based on protected characteristics.
- Do not rank neighborhoods using protected-class proxies.
- Do not provide legal, lending, tax, or fair-housing advice.
- Use approved property and brokerage data sources.
- Record consent and honor opt-outs across channels.
- Escalate representation, agency, offer, contract, and complaint questions.
- Review transcripts and routing for disparate treatment.
- Keep licensed activities with properly licensed people.

## FAQ

### Is an AI ISA the same as an AI receptionist?

No. An ISA workflow responds, qualifies, nurtures, and schedules. A receptionist answers inbound calls and routes broader inquiries. Scale includes both voice-agent and receptionist coverage.

### Can the agent answer listing questions?

It can answer from approved, current sources within defined limits. Material property, representation, offer, and legal questions should route to a licensed person.

### Can it reactivate old real estate leads?

Yes, when the database is segmented, consent and suppression rules are applied, and the message matches the relationship and timeline.

### Does Prestyj also generate leads?

Yes. Current plans include managed Google and Meta ads, included ad spend, landing or website assets, and batch video ad production.
