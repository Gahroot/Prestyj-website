Inbound qualification should give the broker a usable next action without forcing a caller through an unnecessary interrogation. The right checklist captures the commercial requirement, identifies routing constraints and preserves unanswered questions. It does not turn an unverified statement into a verified property fact.

This unreleased draft awaits brokerage, legal and editorial review. It is a proposed intake design, not a claim about conversion rates or deployed calling capabilities.

## Define the assignment before the script

Document which properties, markets and transaction types the workflow covers. Name the responsible brokerage team and the approved source of listing information. Record what the workflow must not discuss or promise, including unsupported availability, negotiated terms and legal interpretations.

Use a human path for requests outside that scope. A caller with an existing representative or an ambiguous assignment should not be pushed into a generic booking flow simply to complete the form. The routing owner determines how those inquiries are handled.

## Minimum-data checklist

| Intake field                    | Why it is useful                                    | How to handle an unknown                      |
| ------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| Property or market of interest  | Selects the relevant assignment                     | Ask for clarification or route by market      |
| Transaction type                | Distinguishes leasing, purchase and other inquiries | Preserve the caller's wording                 |
| Intended commercial use         | Helps the broker assess fit                         | Do not infer from identity or accent          |
| Approximate size                | Narrows the requirement                             | Record a range or declined response           |
| Timing                          | Establishes urgency and next step                   | Mark unconfirmed rather than inventing a date |
| Representation status           | Flags an assignment or coordination question        | Route to a person                             |
| Preferred contact and next step | Supports a permitted handoff                        | Do not assume permission across channels      |

Only collect budget or financing details when the approved workflow genuinely needs them. Do not request account credentials, payment details or unrelated personal information. Keep sensitive material out of general CRM notes and broad notification channels.

## A synthetic qualification record

A fictional caller asks about 8,000 to 10,000 square feet of office space in the assignment market, with a desired move in six months. They have not selected a property and say they are already working with another broker. They prefer a callback and decline to state a budget.

The correct record preserves those facts and unknowns. It does not assign a budget based on market averages, promise that a property is available or classify the inquiry as unqualified solely because a field is missing. The routing reason is the existing-representation question, with a human owner assigned to review it.

If no broker has accepted the callback, the status is awaiting acceptance. If the calendar is unavailable, the status is not booked. This distinction prevents the dashboard from counting an attempted handoff as a completed next step.

## A routing decision table

For an in-scope property with clear assignment, route to the designated team. For a market-only inquiry, use the approved market rule. For existing representation, conflicting assignment or a request for a person, route to human review. For an unsupported language or poor audio, offer the approved alternate path without pretending the qualification is complete.

Never route based on protected or sensitive personal characteristics. The firm's qualified reviewers should assess intake criteria and actual operating context before launch. This article does not determine legal obligations for any particular transaction or jurisdiction.

## Consent and communications are separate gates

An inbound inquiry does not automatically authorize recorded retention, outbound marketing calls, text messages or email campaigns. The applicable requirements depend on locations, purpose, channel and processing behavior. Counsel should review the actual flow and disclosures, not just a generic script.

Record the scope of any permission or refusal where the approved policy requires it. If recording is declined, do not attach a recording or imply a transcript exists. If follow-up permission is uncertain, stop the affected channel and route the question for review. Keep a permitted human contact option where possible.

## Measure handoff quality

Track attempts, accepted handoffs, rejected handoffs, incomplete records and unresolved routing issues. Have brokers state why a record was not actionable. Counting only answered inquiries rewards a workflow that answers quickly but leaves the broker to reconstruct the requirement.

Test with synthetic cases for duplicate contacts, missing consent, stale listing information, an unavailable broker and a caller who changes the requirement mid-conversation. Verify that corrections update the draft record without erasing the prior context needed for review.
