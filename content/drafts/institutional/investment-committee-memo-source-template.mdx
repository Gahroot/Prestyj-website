A committee memo is not improved merely by adding more citations. It improves when a reviewer can tell which claims are established, which depend on assumptions and which remain unresolved. The template below separates those states without turning the memo into a document dump.

This is an unreleased editorial template awaiting investment and editorial review. It is not an investment recommendation or a claim that a committee has accepted the format.

## Put the decision and evidence status on the first page

Start with the requested decision, the property and entity identities, the reporting cutoff and the version of the acquisition criteria. State who prepared the draft and who is responsible for reviewing it. An AI-prepared paragraph is still a draft until the designated person accepts its evidence and interpretation.

The first-page summary should name the unresolved items that could change the decision. Do not place a material missing lease or an unverified financing assumption only in an appendix. The committee needs to know whether it is reviewing an unconditional recommendation, a conditional proposal or a request for more diligence.

## Reusable memo structure

| Section                  | Required contents                                      | Evidence attachment                       |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------- |
| Decision requested       | Action, scope, amount where applicable, decision owner | Approved request and authority            |
| Asset and thesis         | Identity, strategy, fit with acquisition criteria      | Criteria version and asset source IDs     |
| Operating position       | Defined metrics and reporting periods                  | Source rows and reconciliation notes      |
| Underwriting assumptions | Assumption, rationale, sensitivity and owner           | Approved model version and cell locators  |
| Diligence exceptions     | Competing facts, consequence, owner and treatment      | Both supporting and contrary sources      |
| Risks and conditions     | Unresolved dependencies and proposed conditions        | Evidence requests and reviewer decisions  |
| Approval record          | Exact memo version and committee disposition           | Dated decision record, not draft language |

Use the structure proportionately. A short screening memo may not need every schedule, but it must not imply that omitted diligence was completed.

## Claim-to-source table

For each material claim, capture claim ID, exact statement, source ID, source version, locator, effective date, interpretation owner and status. “Source: data room” is not enough. A reviewer needs the page, clause, row or model cell that supports the statement.

Separate source fact from inference. A signed lease might establish a contractual date; whether that date changes the investment thesis is a judgment. Both can appear in the memo, but the evidence trail should show where the factual record ends and the interpretation begins.

Do not copy unrestricted source links into a broadly circulated document. Use a permitted repository location and confirm that the committee can access it. A citation that exposes confidential files to the wrong audience is not an improvement.

## Synthetic worked paragraph

Assume a fictional asset's approved rent roll reports $100,000 of monthly contractual rent at September 30. A signed amendment adds $4,000 starting October 1. The draft model has already included that increase in September.

A useful paragraph says: “The September rent-roll figure is $100,000. The reviewed amendment supports a $4,000 step beginning October, while model version M-03 includes it in September. The asset-management reviewer has requested a timing correction. The committee should treat the September model amount as unresolved pending that correction.”

Attach the rent-roll row, amendment section and model cell under separate source IDs. Do not write “rent is verified at $104,000” merely because that is the largest number in the packet. The example is synthetic and does not assess any real asset.

## Open-item treatment before circulation

Classify each material open item as resolved with evidence, condition proposed, or decision deferred. Record the owner and next required evidence. A blank status must not be converted to “no issue found.” Where committee accepts a condition, retain its exact scope and the person responsible for closing it.

After a source changes, identify every affected claim and create a new memo version. Prior approval belongs to the earlier version. A silent replacement of an appendix makes it difficult to establish what the committee actually reviewed.

## Final reviewer checklist

Confirm identities, periods, units and definitions. Recompute material arithmetic. Open the cited sources rather than trusting their titles. Read contrary evidence and verify that limitations survive the executive summary. Confirm that proposed actions do not exceed delegated authority. Finally, record the reviewed artifact version and permitted recipients.

The published [diligence evidence spine](/blog/evidence-led-real-estate-diligence) provides the broader source-register process. This template focuses on the last mile: a concise decision document whose claims can still be challenged.

## Sources and limitations

This memo structure is proposed editorial guidance. It is not a legal form, investment advice or a substitute for the firm's committee procedures. The financial figures and document identifiers are invented. Actual memos require authorized source records, approved criteria and qualified investment review; those reviews have not been performed for this draft.
